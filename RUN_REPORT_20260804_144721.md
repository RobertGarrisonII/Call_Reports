## 4. Results that ARE from this run's data — Table 9

Table 9 is estimated on the 24 extracted sessions. Of ten shocks × two regimes, four cells reach
significance, and **three of the four change materially between the two correlation estimators**:

| shock | regime | Pearson | HY | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OFI_ES | benchmark | **−0.014\*\*\*** | +0.009 (ns) | **sign flip, significance lost** |
| RV_ES | volatile | 0.006 (ns) | **0.027\*\*\*** | significant only under HY |
| WtdSpread_ES | benchmark | −0.000 (ns) | **−0.030\*\*** | significant only under HY |
| WtdSpread_SPY | benchmark | **0.027\*\*** | **0.032\*\*** | stable — safe to interpret |

This is the both-ways specification test doing exactly what it was built for. The published Table 9
uses grid-sampled Pearson only, and on this sample that estimator **reverses the sign** of the
ES order-flow-imbalance response in the benchmark panel and **conceals** two other effects. Only
`WtdSpread_SPY` survives both estimators unchanged.

The largest Pearson↔HY gaps are on the realized-variance shocks (`RV_SPY` ±0.056/−0.082), which is
where Epps attenuation should bite hardest — attenuation moves with trading intensity, and RV is a
volatility proxy.

**Caveat that applies to the whole table:** it was fitted at VAR(12), and 12 was the search bound
(see §5). Treat the magnitudes as provisional.

## 5. Table 7's correction is the most consequential number in the run

Benchmarked against the independence null **at each aggregation's own order counts** rather than the
per-second null the paper applies to all three rows:

| aggregation | orders/bar (ETF / FUT) | null | observed | obs / null |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 second | 505 / 112 | 0.35 % | 11.88 % | **33.5×** |
| 10 millisecond | 5.05 / 1.12 | 22.88 % | 30.14 % | **1.32×** |
| action time | 1 / 1 | 50.08 % | 48.40 % | **0.97×** |

The tandem effect is a **one-second phenomenon**. At ten milliseconds it is a third above chance;
at action time it is *at or slightly below* chance. Using the per-second null (0.4 %) for all three
rows, as published, turns the bottom two rows into ~75× and ~120× multiples that are entirely an
artifact of the wrong benchmark.

Table 5's corrected view is also worth noting. Against independence *given the observed marginals*:

| panel | PCMOF obs / indep | NCMOF obs / indep | corner log OR (z) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A baseline | 43.1 / 28.6 = 1.51 | 15.7 / 28.6 = 0.55 | 2.017 (52.4) |
| B volatile | 46.5 / 26.0 = 1.79 | 8.3 / 26.0 = 0.32 | **3.449 (69.6)** |
| C MWCB | 21.1 / 14.5 = 1.46 | 9.1 / 14.8 = 0.61 | **1.824 (33.5)** |

Cross-market dependence rises sharply from baseline to volatile (odds ratio 7.5 → 31.5) but **falls
on the MWCB days to below baseline** (6.2). If that survives estimation on the real extracted data,
it cuts against the natural reading that extreme stress maximises tandem trading — and it is
consistent with what the ES tapes showed directly: futures activity *collapses* when the equity
market halts rather than concentrating.

## 6. Action items, ranked

**(1) `validate_aggregated` reported DISAGREEMENT (replay vs ladder), ESH5 on 2024-12-18.**
This is the one genuinely concerning result. The ES leg is now built *from* the venue ladder, and
this check exists to show an independent message replay reproduces it. It did not. Three candidate
causes, in order of likelihood: the comparison ran on **ESH5**, the newly-front contract six days
after the roll carrying ~78 % of volume — a thinner ladder than ESZ4, which is what previously
validated clean; the era-aware family selection picked a family whose replay is incomplete; or a
genuine ladder/replay divergence. **Send `validate_ESH5_20241218.txt`** — the level-1 agreement
percentage and the new "replay built from the X family" line will separate these immediately.

**(2) The ES crossed-book invariant is now vacuous.** `ES_crossed = 0.00 %` on all 24 sessions.
A venue-published ladder cannot cross by construction, so that column no longer tests anything on
the futures side — it is not evidence the ES leg is right. Item (1) is now the *only* check on the
ES book, which raises its priority.

**(3) The halt union may not include the ES windows.** The `halt` column reads 900/900/900/901
snapshots — the SPY circuit-breaker windows alone. On 2020-03-18 the ES leg resumes 6 s *after* the
equity reopen, so the union should be ~906. Worth verifying that `halt_windows_ES` was derived,
because those snapshots are what gets excluded from every pair estimate.

**(4) Memory sizing is over-subscribed.** Measured peak RSS per worker was **58.4 GiB** against the
built-in guess of 32; autoscale recommends `PEAK_GB_GUESS=76`, i.e. **5 workers, not 13**. Thirteen
workers at 58 GiB is 758 GiB nominal against 495 available — this run survived only because the
peaks did not coincide. Export `PEAK_GB_GUESS=76` before the next run.

## 7. Gaps and sample notes

* **No 10 ms frames.** STAGE 5 could not run the fine-grid pair, "which is where the Epps gap should
  be largest". Given that three of four significant Table 9 cells already move between estimators at
  1 s, the 10 ms run is now the highest-value missing piece. Re-run STAGE 2 with `--interval 10ms`.
* **The lag order is at the search bound.** BIC selected p = 12 = pmax with the criterion still
  falling monotonically (36.068 at p=0 → 22.424 at p=12). Per v0.9.45 this is the `corr_window=100`
  artifact: differencing a 100-bar rolling correlation puts an MA term at lag 100, so raising
  `--pmax` walks *toward* 100 rather than converging. **Re-run STAGE 5 with `--with-dcc`** (v0.9.45)
  and quote the DCC column.
* **Six of 24 sessions sit inside an ES roll window** — 2020-03-12 (+0), 2020-03-16 (+4),
  2020-03-18 (+6), 2023-03-09 (+0), 2024-12-18 (+6), 2025-06-13 (+1). The single-contract futures
  leg misses 6–40 % of ES volume on those days. This belongs in the sample appendix.
* **SSR is unreported on five sessions** — 2024-08-05, 2024-08-09, 2024-12-18, 2025-06-13,
  2026-06-05 show `n/r`. Absence is not "unrestricted"; those sessions have no Rule 201 control.
* **LULD bands are 0 % on every session.** Expected for the direct equity feeds. Note that CME
  *does* publish price limits for ES, so the `ES_luld_*` columns should be checked separately — the
  QC table's single `luld_known` column reports the equity leg only.
* **Carried NaN book cells** run 48 k–88 k on the 2020 sessions and ~70 k on the 2025–26 sessions
  (≈2.6 % of cells), against <15 k on most 2023–24 days. Worth a glance at which columns — likely
  deep ladder levels the venue does not always publish.

## 8. Bottom line

The pipeline is sound and the data is the best it has been. The blocking defect is fixed, the
integrity gates are green, and Table 9 already yields a defensible methodological result: the
published estimator reverses one sign and hides two effects.

Before any number here goes into the paper, three things need doing — resolve the ladder/replay
disagreement (§6.1), re-run Table 9 with DCC to escape the boundary lag (§7), and **estimate Tables
5 and 7 on the extracted sessions rather than on the published matrices** (§3). The third is the
largest remaining gap between this run and a replication: 561,624 rows were extracted and the
tandem-trading tables did not touch them.
