II. Data-Quality Findings

D-1 — ES ladder staleness is severe, early-era, and concentrated in the benchmark regime. At 10 ms, 25 of 66 sessions have the ES top of book unchanged on 99.0–99.9 % of snapshots (range 0.9904–0.9994); a further 38 sessions sit between 0.90 and 0.99; the sample minimum is 0.885 (2020-03-18). No session in 2022, 2025, or 2026 exceeds 0.99. At 1 s the worst four sessions (0.92–0.97) are all 2017–2018 benchmark days, including 2017-11-28, the replacement control. The regime asymmetry (64 % of benchmark vs 24 % of volatile sessions stale at the 0.99 threshold) means any staleness-induced bias loads on the benchmark side of every regime contrast. The v0.9.87 measured bias curve (planted truth through the real Tier-1 pipeline) finds attenuation — bias −0.90 z at 85 % staleness, monotone — under the carry-forward mechanism; under that mechanism the benchmark flow correlation is understated and the volatile-minus-benchmark gap is conservative. The refresh-clustering channel (which would push the other way) is not in the simulated curve and remains open. (qc/qc_frames_10ms.txt; qc/qc_frames.txt)

D-2 — The run estimated at 10 ms on sessions its own QC forbids. qc_frames_10ms.txt marks the 25 stale sessions ok=False with the standing instruction "Do NOT estimate on a session that violates its own invariant"; extraction recorded them DEGRADED under qc_action=warn; STAGE 6b, the 10 ms flow stage, and the horizon ladder estimated on all 66. Disposition options: the P0 clean-subsample rerun, the es_stale_frac dose-response covariate (shipped in v0.9.87), or per-exhibit exclusion — but the current record should be read with D-1 in hand. III. Method Findings

M-1 — Clustering is worth a factor of seventy. SPY_ret ~ ES_OFI over 1,529,994 observations: pooled-iid t = 386.87; day-clustered t = 5.56 (wild-bootstrap p = 0.001). The 69.5× deflation is the single most consequential inference fact in the run and governs the reading of every pooled statistic below. (legacy__inference_iid_vs_clustered.csv)

M-2 — The Table 5 z-statistics are bar-count artifacts. log_OR_z of 159.8 / 214.3 / 77.7 / 64.2 across the four panels are Woolf statistics on pooled bars; the z ordering tracks panel size, not effect size (the largest log OR, MWCB 1.470, has near the smallest z). The v0.9.87 day-clustered inference (per-day count matrices; day bootstrap; within-pair contrast; MWCB jackknife) replaces them; on a planted serially-dependent DGP the Woolf z overstates the day-clustered z by ~21×. (nulls/table5_corrected_null.csv)

M-3 — The lag-order record is untidy but internally explicable. STAGE 4c resolved p = 10 (BIC, RealBar frame, 1 s); the Table 9 frames' own BIC selected p = 5; the per-day modal selection is p = 1 on 92–95 % of days; AIC never attains an interior minimum (still falling at the pmax = 30 bound — the footnote-17 artifact seen from below); the VECM stages use per-grid heuristics (5 at 1 s, 60 at 10 ms). Three different "BIC lags" appear in the record with no reconciling exhibit. The v0.9.87 local-projection Table 9 counterpart is lag-order-free and renders the dispute moot for the IRF claims it covers. (replication.log; table9_lag_ic_informational_w100.csv)

M-4 — Identification: the Rigobon column is unusable, by its own diagnostic. Cholesky (futures-first) gives contemporaneous SPY←ES = 0.892; the heteroskedasticity-ID column reverses it (−0.090 / +0.976) but its var-ratio spread is 0.066 (~common-scale, unidentified) and the printed verdict is "NO — do not quote the het-ID column." The Cholesky ordering stands by construction, not by test. Note an annotation defect: the eigenvalue-gap row (0.438) clears its own stated >0.10 threshold while the verdict is NO; the verdict is keyed to the var-ratio spread. (legacy__identification_Cholesky_vs_Rigobon.csv)

M-5 — FEVD vs GFEVD. With measured OFI-innovation correlation 0.705 (benchmark) / 0.788 (volatile), the diagonal FEVD is invalid; the GFEVD attributes 45.7 % of SPY-return variance to ES flow (benchmark) and 50.0 % (volatile) versus 27.9 % / 50.5 % under the diagonal. The shipped FEVD/GFEVD CSVs are the benchmark-regime tables; the volatile-regime decompositions exist only inline in report.md (§ V, E-9). (irf__fevd.csv; irf__gfevd.csv; irf__ofi_innovation_corr.csv; 1s report.md)

M-6 — DCC is a boundary estimate. a + b = 0.99990; mean conditional ρ 0.811 vs realized correlation 0.923. Per convention the realized correlation is the quotable level; DCC's near-zero IRF responses in Table 9 (§ IV.3) should be read against this. (dcc.csv)

M-7 — CS is the fragile statistic, quantified. Mean CS_ES drifts +0.071 (22 %) across VECM lags 3→20 while IS moves −0.014; single-day CS swings up to 0.78 between fixed and estimated cointegrating beta (2021-11-26: 0.024 → 0.801) while the largest IS swing is 0.127; nine (1 s) / ten (10 ms) low-κ days carry the extreme CS values; excluding them nearly eliminates the 1 s CS regime gap. IS is the primary metric throughout. (robustness__lag_sensitivity.csv; robustness__beta_fixed_vs_estimated.csv; information_shares__per_day.csv)

M-8 — Two regime taxonomies coexist. Copula and Table 5 exhibits break MWCB out (volatile = 37); the per-day analysis tables, cross-impact, horizon, geometry, and state-conditioning tables fold MWCB into volatile (= 41). Documented in RUN_NOTES as intended, but every cross-family "volatile" comparison mixes day sets, and the MWCB days are extreme on most measures. (RUN_NOTES.md; per-day tables)

M-9 — Era drift is real and untested. IS_mid_ES rises monotonically across every market-era flag at both grids (e.g., pre/post T+1: 0.447 → 0.492 at 1 s; 0.437 → 0.565 at 10 ms), but era cells are regime-imbalanced (post-tick-2025 contains zero benchmark days) and κ falls simultaneously; no era test ships. Book-geometry levels drift similarly (2017 SPY τ_bid ≈ 0.058 vs 2024–26 ≈ 0.13–0.21). Cross-era level comparisons are confounded without the within-pair design. (information_shares__per_day.csv; book_geometry_per_day_1s_L10.csv)

IV. Findings on the Substantive Questions

F-1 — Tandem flow coupling orders monotonically with stress, on both grids. Tier-1 OFI-innovation correlation: benchmark 0.729 < volatile 0.818 < MWCB 0.870 at 1 s (volatile−benchmark dz +0.226, permutation p = 0.0008); benchmark 0.266 < volatile 0.292 < MWCB 0.355 at 10 ms (dz +0.028, p = 0.0327). This is the only flow exhibit that agrees across grids in direction and significance, and it is the run's most robust substantive result. The staleness bias curve (D-1) says the measured mechanism biases it conservatively. (flow_corr_tier1_.csv)*

F-2 — The asymmetry reversal is a 1 s result; it does not replicate at 10 ms. At 1 s: benchmark sell-side tighter (dz +0.047, sign-flip p = 0.0045); volatile buy-side tighter (dz −0.090, p < 5 × 10⁻⁵); MWCB symmetric (p = 0.62). At 10 ms every group's gap is an order of magnitude smaller and non-significant (volatile dz −0.0023, p = 0.47). The gap-of-gaps (−0.137 at 1 s) is itself untested in the shipped tables. The reversal remains the most novel candidate finding, but the record supports it only at the 1 s aggregation, and the SSR/direction-conditioning checks (memorandum P1; §VI) remain outstanding. (flow_corr_asymmetry_.csv)*

F-3 — Mediation: the mediator link is rock-solid; the decomposition is grid-dependent. The within-bar mediator coefficient (Δz_flow → Δz_ret) is 67.0 (t ≈ 17.8) at 1 s and 75.3 at 10 ms — the strongest single coefficient in the run. The suppression structure (direct +5.71 vs indirect −6.84, total ≈ 0) holds at 1 s, where the indirect level CI [−41.9, −3.14] excludes zero; the share statistic (605 %) is a ratio to a near-zero denominator and should not be quoted. At 10 ms the total is significant (−1.076***), the direct keeps its sign, and the indirect CI includes zero. The two grids agree that RV_ES suppresses flow innovations (Eq A negative both grids) and disagree on the rest. RV_SPY's indirect channel is never reported despite significant Eq-A coefficients on both grids. (flow_corr_mediation_.csv/.md)*

F-4 — Lead-lag: ES leads by ≈ one 20 ms step — at the grid that can see it, and net of staleness. At 10 ms, raw HRY θ = +0.020 s on 62 of 66 days (64 ES-leading; sign-flip p = 0.0005) and the co-jump lead test gives ES-first on 65 of 66 days (mean lead share 0.358, p = 5 × 10⁻⁵; pooled 308,921 ES-first vs 135,777 SPY-first). At 1 s neither test resolves anything (p = 0.52 / 0.96) — a resolution artifact, not a contradiction. Two integrity notes: (i) the co-jump ES-lead share correlates positively with ES update counts (Spearman +0.57), i.e. the result is weakest on the stale sessions and is not a staleness artifact; the two extreme raw-θ days (2017-06-26, 2017-10-05) are the two stalest sessions and should be excluded from θ magnitudes. (ii) The pre-averaged θ column reverses sign on nearly every day (62/66 negative at 10 ms) and ships untested — the raw-vs-preavg contradiction is unadjudicated on this record (§ V, E-2). (lead_lag, jumps__cojump tables)

F-5 — Information shares: parity-to-SPY tilt on average; the regime shift is not era-robust. Mean IS_mid_ES ≈ 0.457 (1 s) / 0.466 (10 ms); CS_ES 0.323 / 0.381. The only sub-0.05 regime result is the 1 s free-permutation IS test (diff +0.032, p = 0.032); the era-robust within-pair test was run only on CS, where it reverses sign at 1 s (mean pair diff −0.002, p = 0.976) and is positive but insignificant at 10 ms (+0.038, p = 0.132). No within-pair IS test exists in this run — the single significant regime result has no era-robust counterpart. v0.9.87 adds the within-pair and ex-straddle tests on IS_mid_ES (regime_p_IS_within_pair), so the next run carries the era-robust verdict on the primary metric. The pooled panel-VECM interaction is significant on the ES equation at both grids (p = 0.0099 / 0.0297) but is pooled, not day-level, inference. (information_shares__regime_test.csv; summary.json)*

F-6 — Tables 5 and 7. Corner concentration: PCMOF observed/null ratio rises 1.243 → 1.280 → 1.372 (baseline → volatile → MWCB) and NCMOF falls 0.763 → 0.730 → 0.659; log OR 0.977 → 1.124 → 1.470 (1.413 ex-SSR); corner asymmetry ≈ −0.02 in every panel (no SSR fingerprint at the pooled level). Table 7: the NCMOF excess over the frequency-matched null collapses from 33.5× (1 s bars) to 1.32× (10 ms) to 0.97 (action time) — the published aggregation story reproduces on this sample. Inference for both awaits the v0.9.87 day-clustered machinery (M-2); Table 7 ships no uncertainty at all. (nulls/table5_corrected_null.csv; nulls/table7_frequency_matched_null.csv)

F-7 — Copulas: three exhibits, three stories, one significant contrast. Returns: tail-dependence level rises with stress (median λ 0.531 benchmark → 0.685 volatile) but no crash-vs-rally asymmetry anywhere (all sign-flip p ≥ 0.125); benchmark days select the symmetric t on 19/25 — which forces λ_L = λ_U, so the parametric asymmetry test is constrained exactly where it matters (remedied by the v0.9.87 nonparametric columns). Flows: volatile days select Frank (zero tail dependence) on 32/37 — extreme flow surprises decouple under stress while returns tighten. Liquidity split: crash-tail dependence is higher in thin-book halves on volatile days, Δλ_L = +0.076, sign-flip p = 5 × 10⁻⁵ — the only significant copula contrast in the bundle, and a direct book-state → tail-risk link. The "median p(upper-tail LR) = 0.0" column is degenerate in all eight groups and contradicts the Frank selections; do not quote it (§ V, E-6). (1s/copula/)*

F-8 — The horizon ladder localizes cross-impact transmission at 250 ms. SPY-from-ES cross-impact rises monotonically 0.038 (10 ms) → 0.370 (1 s); the per-day half-impact horizon lands at 250 ms on 39 of 66 days (median 250 ms in both regimes), but volatile days reach half-impact by 100 ms on 19/41 versus 3/25 benchmark — transmission is faster under stress. The reverse direction (ES-from-SPY) has no defined half-impact horizon (non-monotone, negative benchmark medians); the summary's mean half-life column is outlier-dominated (2024-12-18 contributes 1,000–3,400 s rows) — use the monotone per-day medians (1.7 → 17.4 s). (horizon/)*

F-9 — Cross-impact is one-directional except during MWCB weeks. SPY-return-on-ES-flow is positive and significant on 66 of 66 days (benchmark mean 0.306, volatile 0.409); ES-return-on-SPY-flow is ≈ 0 in benchmark (median −0.009; significantly negative on 14/25 days) and small-positive volatile (0.050) — but switches on, significantly, on all four MWCB days (0.073–0.178, t 3.1–6.7). The pooled "cross" row in the regime summary (0.156 / 0.229) masks this ~50-fold direction asymmetry. (cross_impact__panel.csv)

F-10 — Jumps: a larger share of QV on benchmark days. Common-factor jump fraction 0.350 benchmark vs 0.186 volatile at 1 s (truncation; same ordering per asset and under LM) — volatile days are volatile through the continuous component, not through a higher jump share. The ES share of the jump component is grid-stable (≈ 0.50) while its continuous-component share is grid-dependent (0.43 → 0.24). 10 ms jump fractions (0.78) are staleness-inflated and method-confounded; quote the co-jump lead ratios instead. (jumps.csv)*

F-11 — MS regimes (completed): multi-regime structure is near-universal; alignment with the a-priori labels is qualitative only. 64/66 days multi-regime at 1 s (58/66 at 10 ms); top-regime occupancy orders with the design: benchmark 0.231 < volatile 0.387 < MWCB 0.729. Benchmark low-coupling states are significantly more persistent at 1 s (median durations 156/36 bars, p = 0.00075) — not replicated at 10 ms (p = 0.98). Pooled entry-on-selling (entry return −2.76 bps, p = 0.020) is 1 s-only. No formal state-alignment statistic (confusion matrix, concordance) ships; every "median p(1v2) = 0.01" cell is the 99-draw floor. (flow_corr_ms_regimes_.csv)*

F-12 — The flow-correlation → price-discovery link (pd_link) disagrees across grids in sign. All six 10 ms coefficients positive and significant (IS row +0.0151 to +0.0165**); all 1 s coefficients null with negative CS points. The exhibit conflates grid with specification (VECM lag 5 vs 60). On this record the "tandem flow moves price discovery toward ES" claim is 10 ms-only. (flow_corr_pd_link_.csv)*

F-13 — Book geometry: the redundancy verdicts hold on real data. τ is rank-equivalent to the Herfindahl on every one of 132 asset-days (median Spearman 0.972); incremental R² over the covering set: median 0.002 (τ), 0.001 (DWC). Both ship as diagnostics, not regressors, exactly as designed. (book_geometry_per_day_1s_L10.csv)

F-14 — ECM-SDE: state-dependence of adjustment speed is significant only at 10 ms, and the exhibit carries a unit defect. a₁ t-statistics: 0.30 (1 s mid) vs 3.68 (10 ms mid) / 7.26 (10 ms microprice); microprice raises the implied median ES share at both grids (0.176→0.308; 0.569→0.646). The half_life_s column is computed in bar units at both grids — at 10 ms the printed 137.5 "seconds" is 1.375 s of clock time. The cross-grid ECM-SDE leadership reversal (SPY-dominant at 1 s, ES-dominant at 10 ms) is unreconciled with the grid-stable Hasbrouck IS. (ecm_sde.csv)*

The summary.json cojump_leader field inherits it. E-2 · theta_preavg_s is opposite-signed to raw θ on essentially every informative day at both grids and no inference ships for it; the contradiction is unadjudicated (F-4). E-3 · half_life_s in the ECM-SDE curves is in bar units; the label is wrong by ×100 at 10 ms (F-14). E-4 · The shipped FEVD/GFEVD CSVs are the benchmark-regime tables, unlabeled; volatile-regime decompositions exist only inline in report.md. E-5 · Rigobon verdict annotation: the eigenvalue-gap row passes its own printed threshold while the verdict is NO (M-4). E-6 · Copula "median p(upper-tail LR) = 0.0" in all 8 groups, including groups whose winning family has zero tail dependence by construction; numerically suspect, do not quote. E-7 · Table 5's two independence nulls differ within each panel (unexplained asymmetric benchmark); Table 7's 1 s ratio (33.54) does not reproduce from its own rounded columns (33.94); "NCMOF" denotes different objects in Tables 5 and 7. E-8 · Regime taxonomy differs across table families (M-8); tier-1 MWCB bar counts differ by one across grids (1501/1502); pd_link window counts 852 vs 849. E-9 · The 10 ms pooled panel-VECM regime split (benchmark IS 0.116 / volatile 0.574) is far outside the per-day IS means (0.437 / 0.484) under the same heading, unreconciled. E-10 · The MWCB duration-asymmetry p is bit-identical across grids (0.1263436828158592) while every input differs — shared permutation stream; verify rather than assume coincidence. E-11 · t_delta_es in the 10 ms liquidity-state tables disagrees in sign with the printed q10−q90 spread on 22/66 (depth) and 15/66 (FPCA) days; the column's tested quantity is undefined in the bundle. E-12 · Single-session exhibits (*_session0, liquidity curves) all use 2017-06-26 — a benchmark day with the third-lowest ES update count, the ladder-monotonicity defect (D-3), and the largest pre-averaging outlier; it is unrepresentative in the directions that matter. E-13 · frames/report.md/summary.json are zero-stage placeholders ([null, null] headline) from the frames-build pass; readable as a failure though they are not one. E-14 · The QC narrative asserts futures price limits are reported while its own coverage column reads 0 % on 65/66 sessions (D-8). E-15 · Lag bookkeeping: p = 10 (STAGE 4c) vs p = 5 (Table 9 frames) vs n_lags 5/60 (VECM stages) vs modal per-day p = 1 (M-3).
